## Must fix for ≥ 8

1. **Unify the middle-state staging.** Sheet 6–8 retreats to a centred armillary display surrounded by empty black, between engaging mechanical close-ups and the cathedral. Carry foreground gear arcs and dim receding shafts through these states; make this feel like entering one machine, not presenting an isolated astronomy model.
2. **Give the strike a readable cause and path.** Rhythm 19–20 shows broad coloured gear patches; most intervening frames offer scattered glints. Expose a distinctive pallet contact, then a narrow luminous front crossing adjacent gear faces/bearings with a dark recovery. The audience should recognize a strike without consulting its description. Half-beat stills do not establish propagation or beat-tight motion.
3. **Recover focal hierarchy at the peak.** Sheet 9–12 piles similarly fine rings, teeth and chains around the core; the large foreground planet competes with it in 10–11. Darken/soften distant tiers and simplify crossings around the escapement, leaving one unmistakable illuminated mechanism framed by larger shadowed masses.
4. **Make the lighting less beige and more material-specific.** Sheet 1–4 has dull brown faces; sheet 10 and rhythm 20 wash substantial gear areas peach-white. Use richer palette-derived metallic mid-tones, narrow specular catches and dark tooth roots rather than broad pale face illumination. Preserve black negative space.

- Warm brass now reads substantially better than the baseline olive cast; pink and cool cohort glints break the monochrome.
- Actual thickness, tooth detail, overlapping armillary rings, shaded planets and receding floor provide convincing spatial cues.
- Distinct presets: 3.8 / 12.5 / 19.5 family-track hits per bar, off-beat ticks, long-cycle strikes and the three-bar cohort pattern. Preserve their placement differences, not merely their density.
