2. **Give the peak an authored silhouette.** Sheet 9–12 becomes a narrow, noisy debris funnel with its upper body cut off. Organize larger folded sheets into readable sweeping ribbons around an open core, with dimmer distant pieces and deliberate foreground framing. Preserve storm scale without confetti clutter.
3. **Make crease-snap a structural event.** Rhythm 15–18 and 20–22 show colour patches more clearly than folding. Have a coherent sector visibly close into a sharply different folded profile, hold briefly, then release; the audience should recognize the event without relying on pink illumination. Half-beat stills cannot establish snap timing.
4. **Replace the glowing bottom lump with a paper focal point.** Sheet 10–12 and rhythm 17–18 concentrate brightness in a lime/peach ball. Resolve illuminated folded faces around a dark cavity; keep the hottest light on selected creases rather than washing out their geometry.
5. **Strengthen the quiet composition.** Sheet 1–5 repeats almost the same flat three-star arrangement. Add legible shallow folds and near/far lighting separation while retaining sparse density; a held breakdown should feel like suspended paper, not a tessellation demonstration.
